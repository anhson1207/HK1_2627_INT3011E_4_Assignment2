# Leader L8: dataset fidelity and bounded SAT experiment

Date: 10 November 2026 (Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh). Branch: `dev/leader`.

## Dataset decision

The [59-row classification](../results/leader/l8_dataset_classification.csv) contains every one of the 58 L7 `NEEDS_VERIFICATION` cases plus `mbeause`. Each row records the original Matrix Market and Harwell–Boeing types, structural and value symmetry, triangular storage check, converted candidate graph size/components, conversion rule, NIST page, raw SHA-256, decision, and reason. The audit checks all 113 compressed files against their recorded hashes and cross-checks NIST's independent diagonal/lower/upper/nonzero statistics against the 59 disputed raw coordinate files. All cross-checks agree. The original compressed matrices are not modified.

All 58 ambiguous files declare `general` in Matrix Market and `unsymmetric` in Harwell–Boeing. Of these, 53 are real (`RUA`) and five are pattern-only (`PUA`). Every one has nonzero coordinates on *both* sides of the diagonal and at least one one-sided off-diagonal pair. Thus their asymmetry is structural, not merely a difference in mirrored values, and none is a symmetric matrix stored as one triangle. Of the 53 numerical matrices, 45 also have unequal values at some mirrored coordinate pairs; eight agree in value wherever both directions exist, but still have one-sided coordinates. The five pattern matrices have no values to compare. The candidate graph formed by the undirected union of nonzero off-diagonal coordinates is documented but is **not** admitted to official SAT benchmarking. The source matrix and the paper do not establish whether union, intersection, or another conversion was used. `fs_680_1` and `fs_760_1` also produce disconnected candidate graphs although they are absent from the paper's Table 5.

The paper defines an undirected simple graph and states that all vertices are labeled, but gives no edge-list checksum or rule for unsymmetric matrix entries. [NIST's Matrix Market specification](https://math.nist.gov/MatrixMarket/formats.html) distinguishes general from symmetric storage and says only symmetric, Hermitian, and skew-symmetric coordinate matrices may supply one triangle. The [paper's computational-results section](https://math.nist.gov/~JBernal/DRSA.pdf) establishes the all-vertices convention. Therefore the preprocessing rules supported for `READY` inputs remain: expand declared symmetric triangular storage, ignore numerical weights for unweighted BMP, remove diagonal entries, collapse mirrored duplicates, and preserve source vertex IDs and isolated vertices. These rules do not settle the 58 general unsymmetric cases.

The dataset remains **54 READY, 58 NEEDS_VERIFICATION, 1 INVALID_DATA**. The 58 are retained for later source or author confirmation, not deleted or silently converted to READY.

The 54 `READY` sources were rechecked: 49 declare symmetric or skew-symmetric storage and contain one off-diagonal triangle, while five declare `general` and have fully mirrored off-diagonal nonzero patterns. Three of those five differ in mirrored numerical values (`orsirr_2`, `saylr1`, `sherman4`). Since the paper's benchmark is an unweighted graph defined by nonzero adjacency, numerical disagreement alone does not change its edge set. The new preprocessing regression test covers that distinction.

## `mbeause`

The [NIST source page](https://math.nist.gov/MatrixMarket/data/Harwell-Boeing/econiea/mbeause.html) describes `mbeause` as a real unsymmetric 496×496 matrix. [SuiteSparse HB/mbeause](https://sparse.tamu.edu/HB/mbeause) independently reports 496×496 and 41,063 nonzeros. The checked raw Matrix Market header also says `496 496 41063`; this rules out a simple loader off-by-four error. Its undirected-union candidate graph has 496 vertices, 36,209 edges, five components, a 492-vertex largest component, and isolated source vertices 493–496. The paper's Table 5 explicitly gives 496 total and 492 in the largest component, whereas Table 8 prints `n=492`. The paper says it uses all vertices, so Table 8 may have inherited the largest-component count from earlier work or contain a typo. This remains an inference: no validated 492-vertex source or published permutation explains the discrepancy. `mbeause` stays `INVALID_DATA` for paper-aligned benchmarking; no vertices are removed.

The NIST page's introductory “entries” number (41,603) differs from both its own 41,063-nonzero statistics table and the downloadable Matrix Market file. The audit compares the detailed nonzero statistics, which match the raw file and SuiteSparse, and records the introductory figure separately.

The separate Harwell–Boeing and SuiteSparse binary downloads were unavailable from this environment (HTTP 403 or connection reset). The independent cross-check therefore covers published metadata and NIST's downloadable Matrix Market coordinates, not a bytewise comparison of two downloaded matrix copies.

## L7 result audit

The [16-row L7 audit](../results/leader/l8_l7_run_audit.csv) recomputes the initial incumbent and lower bound from the original checksummed graphs. `bcspwr01` started at UB 8/LB 4 and `bcsstk01` at UB 27/LB 12. Fifteen runs improved the initial incumbent through completed SAT calls; one (`bcsstk01`, Pairwise + Sequential AMO + Repeated) completed zero calls and returned the initial UB 27. None increased the certified lower bound or proved optimality. All 16 ended at the 3-second worker deadline.

L7 did not record exact per-call outcomes. For these particular unit-step runs, unchanged LB and a UB reduction equal to the completed-call count imply that all *completed* calls were SAT. The final interrupted call, if any, is unrecorded and cannot be called SAT or UNSAT. L7 `solver_time_s` also mixed backend setup with solving for Repeated SAT; L8 records completed solving time consistently and traces threshold outcomes explicitly. For TIMEOUT, that sum excludes the interrupted call and is not the whole elapsed time.

## L8 experiment design

The [run plan](../results/leader/l8_sat_plan.csv) uses only `READY` graphs with verified raw hashes. `bcspwr01` (39 vertices, 46 edges) and `bcsstk01` (48 vertices, 176 edges) receive all eight combinations of Pairwise/Order × Pairwise/Sequential AMO × Repeated/Incremental at 3 seconds. `bcspwr01` receives the same eight at 30 seconds; `bcsstk01` receives the four Sequential AMO combinations at 30 seconds. Order + Sequential AMO + Incremental on the harder `bcsstk01` receives a selected 120-second extension. CaDiCaL 195, deterministic initial incumbent, unit-step threshold order, and no reversal symmetry constraint are held fixed within comparisons.

The run uses an Apple M2 (arm64), 16 GiB RAM, macOS 14, Python 3.14.3, PySAT 1.9.dev17, and CaDiCaL 195. `scripts/run_l8_benchmark.py` exposes all three timeouts, a 600-second total batch budget, 100,000-variable and 500,000-clause estimator guards, and a 4096 MiB worker resident-memory cap. The macOS process rejects a finite address-space limit, so the parent samples child RSS, stops a run above the cap, and records the sampled peak. This is a sampled cap, not a strict instantaneous operating-system memory limit. The worker preserves only independently validated incumbents after a timeout. Published Best is a reference number, never an incumbent.

## New results and acceptance

The [29-run CSV](../results/leader/l8_sat_runs.csv) and [JSON call traces](../results/leader/l8_sat_runs.json) contain initial/final bounds, paper references, SAT-found improvement, status, timing, sampled memory, completed calls, outcomes, and CNF sizes. The 29 worker runtimes sum to 488.5 seconds, inside the configured 600-second batch budget. The sampled per-worker peak RSS ranges from 41.8 to 193.6 MiB, below the 4096 MiB cap.

All 29 runs retain valid feasible permutations, and all 29 have a completed SAT call that strictly improves the initial incumbent. Those are **two distinct improved instances**; 29 is a configuration count. Four runs prove **one distinct instance**, `bcspwr01`, optimal at bandwidth 5. Each proof contains a validated SAT model at `k=5` and UNSAT at `k=4`; the paper independently lists LB/Best 5/5. `bcsstk01` reaches 17, one above the paper's Best 16, but the independently certified LB remains 12. The paper's LB 16 is a reference, not used as a certificate. A “100% feasible” rate alone would be misleading because every run begins with a valid incumbent; the separate SAT-found improvement rate is 29/29 runs and 2/2 instances.

Across the 29 traces there are 120 completed calls: 116 SAT and four UNSAT. There are 23 calls interrupted at the deadline and marked UNKNOWN; two other timeout runs ended before an in-flight call could be recorded. The 120-second `bcsstk01` run made the same ten completed SAT improvements as its 30-second counterpart (27→17), then spent the remaining time on `k=16` without a completed answer. Thus the longer timeout did not improve that configuration's UB or LB. On `bcspwr01`, all 3-second runs timed out, while all four Order variants proved optimal at 30 seconds. The Pairwise variants reached UB 5 but timed out at `k=4`.

For the same eight 30-second `bcspwr01` configurations, Order proved optimal in 4/4 and Pairwise in 0/4. The four Order proof times were 16.8–22.7 seconds. On `bcsstk01` at 30 seconds with Sequential AMO, Order reached 19 (Repeated) and 17 (Incremental), while Pairwise reached 21 and 19. This is evidence on these two graphs only. Pairwise AMO versus Sequential Counter is mixed within Order on `bcspwr01`: Repeated took 22.7 versus 18.9 seconds, while Incremental took 16.8 versus 22.0 seconds. Repeated versus Incremental is likewise mixed on `bcspwr01`; Incremental did improve `bcsstk01` more at 30 seconds for both encodings. CNF counts for Repeated describe the last single formula, while Incremental counts include all appended threshold clauses, so they are not directly comparable as one-formula sizes.

The initial analytical bounds are weak relative to the paper references (`bcspwr01` LB 4 vs 5; `bcsstk01` LB 12 vs 16), and deterministic initial incumbents are 8 and 27 versus paper Best 5 and 16. The descending unit-step schedule therefore spends completed SAT calls reducing UB before trying to prove a hard lower threshold. CNF generation is not the dominant measured cost here: its maximum across the 29 runs is 0.327 seconds, whereas wall limits are 3–120 seconds. The trace identifies the difficult `k=4` and `k=16` calls; completed solving-time totals exclude the interrupted call and must not be read as all CPU time. Incremental uses one persistent CaDiCaL solver and retains learned clauses; its measured benefit is configuration-dependent. The L7 four-run reversal-symmetry ablation had no observed objective or proof gain at 3 seconds, so this report makes no speedup claim. A ten-repetition fast-path check found about 4 ms median added wall time from bounded worker startup and monitoring; that check does not isolate overhead during difficult calls. No new search heuristic or SAT algorithm was introduced without supporting evidence.

The Leader deliverable remains **INCOMPLETE** while the 58 unsymmetric graph-conversion rules and `mbeause` paper alignment lack evidence. The bounded SAT experiment itself is valid on the two verified graphs, and one optimum is independently certified. The next dataset handoff needs the original benchmark edge-list construction, source code, author clarification, or published permutations/checksums before the unresolved cases can enter official comparisons. Better validated initial permutations and stronger certified bounds are the main candidates for future runtime work; published Best alone cannot serve as either.
